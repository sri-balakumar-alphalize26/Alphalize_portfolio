The interfaces we build get used all day, often on cheap hardware and patchy connections — a tablet
on a spa reception desk, a phone in a delivery van, a browser on a counter PC. That puts real weight
on the things that are easy to skip: layout that holds up, bundles that stay small, and text that
still reads correctly when the language flips right to left.

You would own front-end work end to end rather than handing designs off, and you will have the
standing to push back on a design that will not survive contact with a real device.
